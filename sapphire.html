Corundum · Blue

Sapphire

Sapphire is corundum coloured by trace iron and titanium — most often associated with blue, though the same mineral produces every colour of corundum aside from red, which is called ruby instead.

The most sought blues sit in a velvety mid-tone — neither so pale that the colour reads thin, nor so dark that it verges on black under indoor light. Kashmir sapphires set the historical benchmark for this balance, though Sri Lanka and Madagascar consistently supply fine material at a more accessible level.

Many sapphires are heat-treated to improve clarity and even out colour zoning, a stable and widely accepted process. Untreated stones are less common and priced accordingly — we're always clear about which is which.
